# Writing

Use ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) and Zinsser's four principles: Simplicity, Brevity, Clarity, Humanity.

- Simplicity: use familiar words and direct verbs. Explain a necessary technical term when it first appears.
- Brevity: remove repetition, filler, and sentences that add no useful information.
- Clarity: give each sentence one main idea. Name the actor. Use one term for one concept. Keep conditions next to the action they control.
- Humanity: write to a capable person. Explain the reason when it helps them decide. Be candid about limits without ceremony.

For procedures, use active voice and imperative verbs. Give one action per step unless the actions form one simple operation. Put prerequisites and conditions before the action. Aim for at most 20 words in instruction sentences and 25 in descriptive sentences. Keep code, commands, identifiers, and exact UI text unchanged. Use approved STE vocabulary where applicable and define necessary domain terms.

State the outcome first. Support factual claims with source or execution evidence. Mark inference, prediction, or uncertainty in the same passage. Do not force headings into short replies. Avoid rhetorical contrasts, inflated adjectives, long dashes, and formulaic conclusions.

For documents, choose tutorial, how-to, reference, or explanation. Link between purposes instead of mixing them. A readability check is assistance, not ASD-STE100 certification. If strict compliance must be certified, validate against the applicable licensed standard and terminology set.
